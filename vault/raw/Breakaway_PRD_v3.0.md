### v3.0 Change Summary

This version reflects a deliberate strategic simplification. The key changes from v2.0:

- Product positioning clarified: Breakaway is a data aggregator and plan adjuster, not a from-scratch AI plan generator. Race-specific coaching templates are selected at onboarding, then adjusted daily by the AI based on incoming data.
- Plan approach confirmed as Option A — Template Library: Breakaway maintains a curated library of race-specific plan templates (e.g. Berkeley Hills Road Race, flat criterium, gravel 100-miler). The AI adjusts workouts within these templates based on wearable inputs. It does not generate periodization from scratch.
- Onboarding redesigned as a structured questionnaire: a defined sequence of race and fitness questions anchors the experience from session one and populates the Athlete Profile.
- Race-specific plan library introduced: each supported race type maps to a template with documented energy system demands. Berkeley Hills Road Race fully specified as the first template.
- Athlete Data Layer formally specified as the primary technical moat: a structured, queryable store of all inputs and training rides designed to pass only relevant context to Claude, minimising token burn and prompt confusion.
- AI role clarified and bounded: Claude receives structured data summaries and returns targeted plan adjustments and coaching notes. Raw data is never passed directly. Claude never generates periodization plans from scratch.

## 1. Executive Summary

Amateur cyclists training seriously for races face a compounding fragmentation problem: body data lives in Whoop, training load lives in Strava, nutrition is scattered, and the coaching plan lives in a PDF or a TrainerRoad calendar. No platform synthesises these signals into concrete, daily adjustments to an actual training plan.

Breakaway solves this by acting as an intelligent data aggregator and plan adjuster. The athlete selects their target race through a structured onboarding questionnaire. Breakaway assigns a proven, race-specific coaching template. Every day, it pulls data from connected wearables, evaluates it against the template, and through Max — its AI coaching layer — recommends specific adjustments in plain English. The athlete follows a real plan, not a stream of generic suggestions.

Critically, the plan is not generated by AI from scratch. It is a battle-tested template, curated by domain experts, that the AI makes smarter by adjusting it to the individual athlete's daily reality. This distinction matters: it is faster to build, easier to validate, lower in hallucination risk, and more trustworthy to the user.

The defensible moat is not the integrations — those are replicable. The moat is the Athlete Data Layer: a structured, queryable store of every wearable input and training ride, purpose-built to feed Claude only the precise context it needs. Over time, this layer builds a longitudinal model of the athlete that no competitor can replicate on day one.

> [!note] One-line positioning
> Breakaway aggregates your wearable data, maps it to a race-specific coaching template, and tells you exactly what to change — today.

## 2. Problem Statement

### 2.1 The Fragmentation Problem

A competitive amateur cyclist today uses multiple disconnected tools, each doing one thing well in isolation:

| Tool | What It Does Well | What It Cannot Do |
|---|---|---|
| TrainerRoad | Structured workouts, FTP progression, plan templates | Knows nothing about your recovery, nutrition, or life this week |
| Whoop | HRV, sleep staging, recovery score | Has no idea what your plan says or whether today's workout is appropriate |
| Garmin Connect | Power data, training load, VO₂ Max trend | Cannot adjust the plan based on how recovered you are |
| Strava | Activity history, segment data, TSS | No coaching layer, no plan intelligence, no recommendations |
| Nutrition apps | Calorie and macro logging | Completely disconnected from training demands or race timing |

The athlete is manually doing the synthesis that a coach would do: reading across all these tools, deciding whether today's planned workout still makes sense, and making a call. This is cognitively expensive and most athletes get it wrong.

### 2.2 The Missing Layer

What is absent is not more data. Athletes have more data than they can process. What is absent is a layer that sits above the data and translates it into a single, actionable daily recommendation tied to a specific race goal. That is the job Breakaway does.

### 2.3 Why Race-Specific Plans Matter

Generic training plans are built for an average athlete with an average race. Berkeley Hills Road Race is not average. It is three laps of sustained threshold climbing followed by a VO₂ Max sprint to the line. A plan built for a flat criterium will produce an athlete who is fast but cannot sustain power for 20 minutes on a climb. Race-specific templates fix this at the foundation.

### 2.4 User Persona

Primary user: A competitive amateur cyclist preparing for a target road race or gran fondo. Trains 8–12 hours per week. Uses at least one of Whoop, Garmin, or Strava. Has a plan — either from TrainerRoad, a coach PDF, or a generic online source — but has no reliable way to adjust it in real time based on how their body is actually responding. Wants one clear answer every morning: should I do what the plan says today, and if not, what instead?

## 3. Product Vision & Goals

### 3.1 Vision Statement

To be the intelligent layer between an athlete's wearable data and their coaching plan — making every serious amateur's training as adaptive and race-specific as if they had a professional coach reviewing their data every morning.

### 3.2 Product Positioning

Breakaway is a data aggregator and plan adjuster. It is not a training app, a social platform, or a workout library. It is the AI layer that makes a proven coaching plan responsive to the actual athlete following it.

The plan is predetermined and race-specific. The intelligence is in the daily adjustment. The moat is the structured data layer that makes those adjustments increasingly precise over time.

### 3.3 What Breakaway Is and Is Not

| Breakaway IS | Breakaway IS NOT |

| A data aggregator across wearable platforms | A workout streaming app |
| A race-specific plan template adjuster | An AI plan generator from scratch |

| A race strategy and fuelling advisor | A nutrition logging app (native logger is a fallback only) |

## 4. Race-Specific Onboarding Questionnaire

The onboarding questionnaire is the foundation of everything Breakaway does. It is not a settings screen. It is a structured intake that populates the Athlete Profile, selects the correct coaching template, and gives Max the context needed to make every subsequent recommendation meaningful.

The questionnaire is completed once at setup, with individual fields updateable later. It is designed to take under 5 minutes. Every question has a direct downstream effect on the plan or the coaching logic — nothing is collected that is not used.

> [!note] Design principle
> No question appears in the onboarding flow unless its answer changes the coaching output. If removing a question would not change anything Max does, the question does not belong here.

### 4.1 Question Sequence

#### Block 1: Race Target

These questions select the coaching template and set the entire training objective.

| Question | Input Type | How It Is Used |
|---|---|---|
| What is your target race? | Dropdown from supported race library, or manual entry | Selects the coaching template. Each supported race has a documented energy system profile. |
| What is the race date? | Date picker | Sets the training timeline. Determines number of build weeks, taper start, and race week protocol activation. |
| Is this your A-race (peak priority) or a B/C-race? | Single select: A / B / C | A-race gets full taper and race week protocol. B/C-races are trained through or with partial taper. |
| Have you done this race before? | Yes / No | If yes, prompts for prior finish time and any notes. Feeds into the race strategy pacing model. |

#### Block 2: Current Fitness

These questions calibrate the template intensity to the athlete's actual fitness level.

| Question | Input Type | How It Is Used |
|---|---|---|
| Do you know your current FTP (Functional Threshold Power)? | Yes → number entry. No → skip. | Primary intensity calibrator. All training zones and workout targets are expressed as % of FTP. If unknown, estimated from RPE data in first 2 weeks. |
| How many hours per week can you train? | Number slider (4–16 hrs) | Sets total weekly training stress budget. Determines which template volume tier is assigned. |
| Which days are you available to train? | Multi-select day picker | Distributes template workouts across available days. Hard days are not placed on consecutive days unless unavoidable. |
| What is your experience level? | Beginner / Intermediate / Advanced / Category racer | Modifies template intensity and workout complexity. Beginners receive fewer VO₂ Max sessions; advanced athletes receive more race-specific specificity. |

#### Block 3: Connected Platforms

These questions determine which data sources are available, which in turn determines the confidence level of Max’s recommendations.

| Question | Input Type | How It Is Used |
|---|---|---|
| Which platforms do you use? | Visual checkbox menu with device logos | Triggers OAuth connection flow for each selected platform. Determines which data dimensions have high vs. low confidence signals. |
| Do you train with a power meter? | Yes / No | If yes, TSS is power-based (more accurate). If no, TSS is estimated from HR. This affects how conservatively Max interprets training load. |
| Do you have a Whoop? | Yes (already selected above) / No | If no, recovery signal falls back to Garmin body battery or manual 1–10 slider. Max communicates lower confidence in recovery recommendations. |

#### Block 4: Race-Specific Context

These questions appear only for the athlete’s selected race type and feed the race strategy engine.

| Question | Input Type | How It Is Used |
|---|---|---|
| What is your goal for this race? | Dropdown: Finish / Top half of field / Podium / Personal best time / Specific finish time | Sets the ambition level. Affects pacing targets, how conservatively the taper is managed, and the race strategy framing. |
| Do you have the course GPX file? | Upload (optional) | If uploaded, Max parses gradient and identifies key climb profiles. If not uploaded, the template uses the pre-loaded course profile from the race library. |
| Any injuries or physical limitations we should know about? | Free text (optional) | Feeds into Max’s context. High-impact injuries (hamstring, knee) suppress certain workout types in the recommendation logic. |
| How would you describe your biggest limiter? | Dropdown: Sustained climbing / Short punchy efforts / Endurance / Sprint / All-around | Adds a second layer of specificity to workout selection within the template. A climber with poor sprint will receive more anaerobic work than the base template. |

## 5. Coaching Plan Template Library

The template library is the coaching foundation of Breakaway. Each template is a race-specific, periodized training block built by domain experts for a defined race profile. The AI does not generate these plans — it selects and adjusts them.

> [!note] Option A confirmed
> Templates are pre-built by coaches or domain experts and stored in the system. The AI's role is to select the correct template at onboarding and make daily adjustments to it based on incoming data. This approach is faster to build, easier to validate, and more trustworthy to users than AI-generated periodization.

### 5.1 Template Selection Logic

At the end of onboarding, Breakaway selects a template using a two-step process:

- Step 1 — Race type match: the target race is mapped to a race profile category (sustained climbing road race, flat criterium, rolling gran fondo, punchy circuit race, gravel endurance). Each category has one or more templates.
- Step 2 — Volume tier match: the template is scaled to the athlete’s available weekly hours. Templates exist in three tiers: Low (4–8 hrs/week), Mid (8–12 hrs/week), High (12–16 hrs/week). The correct tier is selected automatically from the onboarding answer.

The selected template is displayed to the athlete with a plain-English explanation of why it was chosen and what it is designed to achieve.

### 5.2 Template Structure

Every template follows the same periodization architecture:

| Phase | Duration | Primary Objective | Dominant Workout Types |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | Weeks 1–3 | Aerobic foundation, fatigue resistance | Zone 2 endurance, sweet spot, aerobic tempo |
| Build | Weeks 4–6 | Race-specific intensity, threshold development | Threshold intervals, sustained climbing simulation |
| Speciality | Weeks 7–8 | Peak race-specific fitness | VO₂ Max, race-pace efforts, climbing repeats |
| Taper | Week 9 (race −7 to race day) | Freshness, activation, race readiness | Short intensity, activation rides, openers |
| Recovery | Embedded every 4th week | Adaptation, injury prevention | Zone 1–2, reduced volume, mobility |

### 5.3 Phase 1 Template: Berkeley Hills Road Race

The Berkeley Hills Road Race template is the first fully specified template in the library and the primary product validation vehicle. It is built around the race’s documented energy system demands.

#### Race Profile: Berkeley Hills Road Race

| Race Attribute | Specification |
|---|---|
| Distance | ~60 miles |
| Elevation | ~5,800ft total climbing |
| Format | 3 laps of the same circuit |
| Key climb | Wildcat Canyon Road — 4.2 miles, average 6% gradient, sustained effort |
| Decisive moment | Final lap sprint on South Park Drive after three rounds of threshold climbing |
| Primary energy system | Sustained threshold (FTP 85–95% for 15–20 min repeats) |
| Secondary energy system | VO₂ Max (race-decisive sprint, 3–5 min maximal effort) |
| Key limiter | Ability to maintain threshold power on climbs 2 and 3 after fatiguing on climb 1 |
| Estimated finish time (advanced) | 3:00–3:40 depending on category |

#### Template Workout Emphasis (Mid-Volume Tier, 10 hrs/week)

| Phase | Weekly TSS Target | Key Session Types | TrainerRoad Workout Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base (Wk 1–3) | 380–420 TSS | 2x Zone 2 endurance, 1x sweet spot, 1x long ride | Pettit, Antelope, Polar Bear |
| Build (Wk 4–6) | 440–500 TSS | 2x threshold, 1x climbing simulation, 1x endurance | Galena, Leconte, Carillon |
| Speciality (Wk 7–8) | 460–520 TSS | 1x threshold, 1x VO₂ Max, 1x race-pace climbing | Galena +2, Dade, Bashful +6 |
| Taper (Wk 9) | 220–260 TSS | Activation ride, short openers, rest | Taku, Truuli, rest days |

### 5.4 Phase 2 Template Additions

The following templates are planned for Phase 2, following validation of the Berkeley Hills template:

- Flat criterium — emphasises anaerobic capacity, repeated sprint recovery, and neuromuscular power
- Rolling gran fondo (80–120 miles) — emphasises aerobic endurance, pacing discipline, and fuelling
- Punchy circuit race — emphasises short power, VO₂ Max, and tactical positioning fitness
- Gravel endurance (60–100 miles) — emphasises fatigue resistance, mixed terrain power, and nutrition execution

## 6. Daily Coaching Experience

The daily experience is the product. Every other feature exists to make this moment better. The athlete opens Breakaway each morning and gets one clear answer: here is what the plan says, here is what your body says, and here is what I recommend.

### 6.1 Training Readiness Score

A single 0–100 score synthesising all available signals into one daily verdict. Displayed as the hero element of the dashboard, with a GO HARD / MODIFIED / RECOVER verdict.

| Input Signal | Weight | Source | Fallback if Unavailable |
|---|---|---|---|
| Recovery / HRV | 45% | Whoop (primary), Garmin body battery (secondary) | Manual 1–10 slider, on-demand only — never prompted |
| Training Load Balance | 35% | CTL vs. ATL from Strava + Garmin power data | Strava HR-based estimate |
| Nutrition / Carb Availability | 20% | Native macro logger | Estimated from training load if nothing logged |

The score is recalculated live when nutrition is logged. Max’s recommendation is gated by the score: a score below 50 suppresses hard workouts regardless of what the template prescribes for that day.

### 6.2 Max Coaching Note

Two sentences, delivered every morning, directly below the readiness score. The first sentence states what was planned and what is being recommended instead (if different). The second sentence gives the one most important reason why.

> Planned: Galena — 90 min threshold. Today’s recommendation: Pettit — 60 min Zone 2.
> Your Whoop recovery is 61% and you’ve accumulated 412 TSS this week — going hard today risks the quality of Saturday’s key session.

If the plan is unchanged, the note confirms this and briefly explains what today’s workout is building toward in the context of the race:

> On track: Galena — 90 min threshold, as planned.
> This session simulates the sustained climbing demand of Wildcat Canyon — three weeks out, this is exactly the stimulus you need.

### 6.3 Contextual Reply Chips

Below the coaching note, Max surfaces 3 dynamically generated follow-up prompts based on today’s actual data. These are generated by Claude alongside the coaching note as a structured JSON field, ensuring they are always relevant to what Max just said.

- Tier 1 — Daily contextual chips: generated from today’s data (e.g. “Why Zone 2 today?” appears only because recovery is flagged)
- Tier 2 — Follow-on chips: appear after Max responds to guide the next question
- Tier 3 — Persistent utility chips: always available via scroll — Week ahead, Race readiness, Fuelling plan, Adjust schedule

Maximum 3 chips visible at any time.

### 6.4 Full Chat with Max

Tapping the coaching note opens a full conversational interface with Max. Max has access to the athlete’s structured data context for the session and can answer detailed questions about the plan, the race, fuelling, or any adjustment. All previous sessions are summarised in the Athlete Data Layer and available as context.

### 6.5 Manual Sleep Input

Available on-demand for users without a sleep tracker. Accessible via a single tap from the dashboard. Never proactively prompted. Asks only two questions: hours slept and a 1–10 quality rating. Data is logged to the Athlete Data Layer and incorporated into the next readiness score calculation.

## 7. Athlete Data Layer — The Technical Moat

> [!note] Primary moat
> The Athlete Data Layer is Breakaway’s most defensible asset. It is a structured, queryable store of every input and training ride, purpose-built to pass only the precise context Claude needs for each query. This minimises token burn, eliminates prompt confusion, and compounds in value with every week of use.

### 7.1 Why This Architecture Matters

Most AI fitness products fail at the data layer. They either pass everything into a single large prompt (expensive, slow, and confusing to the model) or pass nothing useful (producing generic advice). Neither is acceptable at scale or in a real product.

Breakaway solves this by treating the athlete’s data as a structured database, not a conversation history. Claude is never asked to reason across raw API responses. It receives a compressed, purpose-built context object assembled from the Athlete Data Layer immediately before each query. The result is faster responses, lower costs, and more precise coaching.

### 7.2 Data Layer Structure

The Athlete Data Layer is a PostgreSQL database with the following primary tables:

#### athlete_profile

Static and semi-static athlete attributes populated at onboarding and updated manually. Fields include all Athlete Profile outputs from Section 4.2, plus: FTP history (date-stamped), injury log, goal history, and preferred workout times.

#### daily_inputs

One row per calendar day. Populated automatically from connected wearable APIs and manually from the nutrition logger. Key fields:

| Field | Source | Data Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| date | System | Date | Primary key for all daily lookups |
| whoop_recovery | Whoop API | Integer 0–100 | Null if Whoop not connected |
| whoop_hrv | Whoop API | Float (ms) | Null if Whoop not connected |
| whoop_sleep_hrs | Whoop API | Float | Falls back to manual_sleep_hrs if null |
| manual_sleep_hrs | User input | Float | On-demand only. Never prompted. |
| manual_sleep_quality | User input | Integer 1–10 | On-demand only. Never prompted. |
| garmin_body_battery | Garmin API | Integer 0–100 | Used as recovery fallback if Whoop null |
| garmin_vo2max | Garmin API | Float | Updated weekly. Used for fitness trend. |
| carbs_logged_g | Native logger | Integer | 0 if nothing logged |
| protein_logged_g | Native logger | Integer | 0 if nothing logged |
| calories_logged | Native logger | Integer | 0 if nothing logged |
| readiness_score | Computed | Integer 0–100 | Calculated from weighted inputs at query time |

#### training_rides

One row per completed activity, populated from Strava webhook. Key fields:

| Field | Source | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| date | Strava | Activity date |
| strava_activity_id | Strava | Primary key for deduplication |
| tss | Computed | Power-based if power meter present, HR-based otherwise |
| normalised_power | Strava | Null if no power meter |
| duration_hrs | Strava |  |
| elevation_gain_m | Strava |  |
| avg_hr | Strava |  |
| planned_workout_id | Template | Foreign key to plan_workouts table. Null if unplanned ride. |
| adherence_flag | Computed | COMPLETED / MODIFIED / SKIPPED / UNPLANNED |
| max_note | System | Max’s post-ride observation if generated |

#### plan_workouts

The current coaching template, exploded into individual workout rows. One row per scheduled workout in the training block:

| Field | Notes |
|---|---|
| date | Scheduled date for this workout |
| template_id | Foreign key to the coaching template library |
| phase | Base / Build / Speciality / Taper / Recovery |
| week_number | Week within the training block |
| workout_type | Zone 2 / Threshold / VO₂ Max / Anaerobic / Recovery / Long Ride |
| trainerroad_workout_name | Named TR workout suggestion (e.g. Galena, Pettit, Dade) |
| trainerroad_link | Direct URL to TrainerRoad workout |
| target_tss | Expected TSS if completed as prescribed |
| target_duration_hrs | Expected duration |
| notes | Coach note on purpose of this workout in the training block |
| status | UPCOMING / COMPLETED / MODIFIED / SKIPPED — updated from training_rides |

#### context_summaries

Pre-computed summary objects that are passed to Claude instead of raw table data. Recomputed nightly and on-demand. The key innovation of the data layer: Claude never queries raw tables directly.

| Summary Object | Contents | When Passed to Claude |
|---|---|---|
| daily_context | Today’s readiness score, recovery signal + confidence level, training load status, carb availability, planned workout, any flags | Every morning coaching note generation |
| weekly_context | 7-day TSS, CTL, ATL, TSB, adherence rate for the week, training phase, weeks to race | Weekly plan review, any adaptive replanning query |
| race_context | Race profile, course demands, weeks out, taper status, race goal, prior result if available | Race strategy queries, pre-race plan generation, race week protocol |
| memory_digest | Compressed summary of key longitudinal patterns: compliance trends, workout types athlete avoids, identified physiological tendencies, historical race data | Any query where individual context materially changes the recommendation |
| nutrition_context | Today’s logged macros vs. targets, carb availability flag, training demand for today | Fuelling advice, pre/post-ride nutrition guidance |

### 7.3 Claude Prompting Architecture

This is the critical design decision that separates Breakaway from naive AI fitness products. Claude is never given a raw data dump. It receives a structured prompt assembled from pre-computed context summaries. A typical daily coaching query looks like this:

```text
ATHLETE PROFILE (static)
FTP: 250W | Race: Berkeley Hills Road Race | Date: April 26 | Weeks out: 3 | Goal: Top half of field | Limiter: Sustained climbing

DAILY CONTEXT (from daily_inputs, computed)
Readiness: 61/100 (MODIFIED) | Recovery: Whoop 61% HRV 52ms [HIGH confidence] | Sleep: 7.2h [HIGH confidence] | Carbs: 148g / 290g target [LOW confidence — manual] | CTL: 68 | ATL: 74 | TSB: -6

PLAN CONTEXT
Planned today: Galena — 90min threshold (TSS 95) | Phase: Speciality Week 1 | Template: Berkeley Hills Mid-Volume

MEMORY DIGEST
Compliance: 82% over last 4 weeks. Tends to under-fuel on hard days. No injury flags active.

QUERY
Generate today’s coaching note (2 sentences max) and 3 contextual reply chip suggestions (JSON array). If readiness < 65, recommend a modified workout from the template’s approved substitution list.
```

This prompt structure has several deliberate properties:

- Context is always labelled by type and confidence level — Max knows when to hedge
- Raw numbers are never passed without interpretation (e.g. CTL/ATL are given, not the full 42-day time series)
- The query is bounded: Max is asked to do one specific thing, not ‘analyse my training’
- Token count is predictable and capped: the daily context prompt is approximately 350–450 tokens regardless of how much underlying data exists
- Claude never touches the database directly — only the pre-assembled context objects

### 7.4 Longitudinal Memory — The Compounding Moat

The memory_digest object is what transforms Breakaway from a tool into a coaching relationship. It is updated weekly by a background process that analyses the training_rides and daily_inputs tables and extracts meaningful patterns:

- Compliance patterns: which days and workout types the athlete consistently completes or skips
- Physiological response curves: how many days HRV takes to recover after different effort types
- Fuelling tendencies: days where calorie/carb intake correlates with poor performance
- Race history: complete record of each race with pre-race plan, actual power file, and outcome

The memory_digest is compressed to under 200 tokens but contains the most diagnostically relevant patterns for the current training context. A race-week session gets race history and taper patterns. A recovery day session gets fatigue response curves.

This is the switching cost. Rebuilding this digest from scratch takes 6–8 weeks of data. No competitor who acquires a new user has it on day one.

## 8. Race & Fuelling Strategy

Race strategy and fuelling guidance are delivered in the lead-up to the target race. This is one of the most differentiated features in Breakaway — no existing platform synthesises current fitness, course demands, and individual data into a concrete, personalised race plan.

### 8.1 Pre-Race Plan (Generated 5–7 Days Before Race)

Max generates a race plan document the athlete can print or screenshot and take to the start line. It is populated from the race_context summary object and the athlete’s current fitness data:

- Target watts by course segment, expressed as % of current FTP
- Pacing strategy narrative: where to conserve, where to push, where this specific race is typically won
- Key decision points by kilometre or mile marker
- Weather note if race-day forecast is available

Example output — Berkeley Hills Road Race:

> Your CTL is 71, TSB is +4 — you’re in good form. Target 87% FTP on Wildcat Canyon (laps 1 and 2). Sit in for the first 10 miles; the pace will feel easy — let it. The race fractures at the base of lap 3’s climb. That’s your moment. Push to 93% FTP from the bottom and do not respond to attacks before KM 28. The sprint on South Park Drive is 3–5 minutes — treat it as a VO₂ Max effort, everything you have left.

### 8.2 Fuelling Plan

Max generates a personalised fuelling schedule based on race duration estimate, individual carb metabolism patterns from the nutrition log history, and standard endurance fuelling guidelines. It is expressed in concrete, practical terms:

- Pre-race meal: timing, carbohydrate target, example foods
- On-bike fuelling: gel and bottle timing by approximate race time (e.g. first gel at 45 minutes, carb bottle sips every 20 minutes)
- Target carbohydrate intake per hour during the race
- Post-race recovery nutrition window and targets

Example:

> Race morning — 3 hours before: 120g carbs. 15 min before gun: 1 gel. On bike: gel at 45 min, gel at 1:20, carb bottle throughout. Target 65g carbs/hour once underway. You tend to under-fuel on hard days — set a phone reminder for each gel.

The final sentence of the fuelling plan example above (“you tend to under-fuel”) is drawn from the memory_digest. This is the feature that makes it feel like a real coach — one who has been watching.

### 8.3 Race Week Protocol (Activates 7 Days Before Race)

Day-by-day taper guidance for the final week:

| Day | Focus | Max’s Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Race −7 (Mon) | Final quality session | Short threshold effort — maintain sharpness, do not add fatigue. Galena − 2 or equivalent. |
| Race −6 (Tue) | Active recovery | Zone 1 spin, 30–45 min. Legs only, no efforts. |
| Race −5 (Wed) | Race openers | Short VO₂ Max efforts — 3x3 min at 110% FTP. Wake the engine. |
| Race −4 (Thu) | Rest | Complete rest or gentle walk. Prioritise sleep. |
| Race −3 (Fri) | Travel / logistics | Rest. Recon the course start area if possible. Carb loading begins tonight. |
| Race −2 (Sat) | Activation | 20–30 min easy spin with 3x30 sec race-pace efforts. Bike check. |
| Race −1 (Sun) | Rest | Rest completely. Final carb-loading meal. Sleep priority. |
| Race Day | Execute the plan | Follow the pre-race plan. Trust the build. |

### 8.4 Post-Race Analysis

After the race, Max detects the Strava activity (flagged as race type by duration and effort level) and generates a brief analysis:

- Power file vs. pre-race plan: where execution matched, where it deviated
- Identified limiters from the data: fitness ceiling, fuelling issue, tactical errors, pacing
- Key learnings stored in the memory_digest for the next race cycle

Each completed race makes the next race plan smarter. This is the flywheel.

## 9. Data Integrations

### 9.1 Signal Hierarchy

When multiple sources provide data for the same dimension, a defined hierarchy governs which signal is used. Signal selection is based on published accuracy research, product market sentiment, and what each platform was built to optimise for.

| Dimension | Primary | Secondary | Fallback | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| HRV / Recovery | Whoop | Garmin body battery (lower weight) | Manual 1–10 slider | Whoop’s algorithm is optimised for overnight HRV during slow-wave sleep. It is Whoop’s core IP. Garmin measures HRV opportunistically. |
| Sleep | Whoop | Garmin sleep tracking | On-demand manual entry — never prompted | Whoop provides sleep staging. Manual input is available but never solicited. |
| VO₂ Max / Fitness trend | Garmin (Firstbeat) | None | FTP-based estimate | Garmin’s Firstbeat algorithm is validated against lab VO₂ Max. Whoop does not attempt this. |
| Training load (TSS) | Garmin via Strava (power) | Garmin HR-based via Strava | Duration + RPE | Power-based TSS is objective. HR-based slightly overestimates intensity. |
| Nutrition / glucose | Native logger | None | Generic population model | Native logger for Phase 1. Lingo CGM as Phase 3 integration. |

### 9.2 Integration Stack

| Source | Data Used | Method | Feasibility | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whoop | Recovery %, HRV, sleep staging, strain | Whoop Developer API v2 (OAuth 2.0) — free, open | Easy | Phase 1 |
| Strava | All activities, TSS, power — aggregates Garmin + TrainerRoad auto-sync | Strava API (OAuth 2.0) | Easy | Phase 1 |
| Garmin Connect | Direct VO₂ Max trend, independent HRV, higher-resolution power files | Garmin Developer Program — application required (~2 days) | Gated | Phase 2 |
| TrainerRoad | Workout name + link recommendations only. No data pulled. | No API. Internal workout database. | No dependency | Phase 1 |
| Native Nutrition Logger | Carbs, protein, fat, calories — quick-add chips + manual entry | Built in-house | Built in-house | Phase 1 |
| Google Calendar | Life events, travel — for life-aware replanning | Google Calendar API (OAuth 2.0) | Easy | Phase 3 |
| Lingo CGM (Abbott) | Real-time glucose, glycemic response | Restricted API. Waitlist only. | Blocked | Phase 3 |
| MyFitnessPal | Full nutrition diary | API closed to new developers | Unavailable | Dropped |

### 9.3 Why Strava Is the Right Primary Pipe (Phase 1)

Strava acts as a universal aggregator for training data, making it more valuable than a direct Garmin integration for Phase 1: Garmin auto-syncs every activity to Strava, so pulling from Strava captures all Garmin data without the Garmin approval process. TrainerRoad completed workouts also auto-sync to Strava, meaning Breakaway sees actual workout completion without any TrainerRoad API dependency. In Phase 2, Garmin direct integration adds VO₂ Max trending and independent HRV data.

## 10. Technical Architecture

### 10.1 Stack

| Layer | Technology | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Frontend | React + Tailwind CSS | Fast prototyping, mobile-friendly, component-based |

| Hosting | Vercel (frontend) + Railway (backend) | Simple deployment for MVP |

### 10.2 Data Flow

The data flow is designed to keep Claude’s context clean and predictable:

- Wearable APIs (Whoop, Strava) push or are polled nightly. Data is written to daily_inputs and training_rides tables.
- Background jobs recompute context_summaries nightly: daily_context, weekly_context, race_context, memory_digest, nutrition_context.
- On user open, the app reads the pre-computed daily_context summary and displays the dashboard. No Claude call is made at this point.
- When the coaching note is requested (either automatically each morning or manually), the backend assembles the structured prompt from the relevant context summaries and makes a single Claude API call.
- Claude returns a JSON response: coaching_note (string, max 2 sentences), modified_workout (object or null), reply_chips (array of 3 strings).
- The response is cached for the day. Subsequent chat messages with Max use the cached coaching note as context and extend the conversation without re-fetching summaries.

### 10.3 Token Budget

Token efficiency is a first-class design constraint. The target is a predictable, capped prompt size regardless of how much underlying data exists:

| Context Object | Estimated Tokens | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Athlete profile (static) | ~80 tokens | Rarely changes. Cached. |
| Daily context | ~120 tokens | Recomputed nightly. |
| Weekly context | ~80 tokens | Recomputed nightly. |
| Race context | ~100 tokens | Changes only as race approaches. |
| Memory digest | ~150 tokens | Compressed pattern summary. Updated weekly. |
| Query instruction | ~50 tokens | Specific, bounded task description. |
| Total daily coaching prompt | ~580 tokens | Well within efficient range. Leaves ample room for Claude’s response. |

For longer conversational sessions (full chat with Max), the conversation history is passed in addition to the context objects. A hard cap of 4,000 tokens total per session is enforced. Older conversation turns are summarised and replaced rather than dropped raw.

## 12. Risks & Open Questions

### 12.1 API Risks

| Integration | Status | Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whoop API | Open, free, active changelog | Low | Proceed |
| Strava API | Open, well-documented | Low | Monitor policy. Tightened for some data types in late 2024. |
| Garmin Connect | Requires developer application (~2 days) | Medium | Deferred to Phase 2. Strava covers Garmin data for Phase 1. |
| TrainerRoad | No API. Recommendation-only. | Low | Only risk: workout renames. Monitor periodically. |
| Lingo CGM | API restricted, not publicly available | Blocked | Phase 3. Waitlist capture in onboarding. Monitor for API opening. |
| MyFitnessPal | API closed | Resolved | Dropped. Native logger built instead. |

### 12.2 Product Risks

- Template accuracy: coaching templates must be reviewed by a domain expert (coach or experienced racer) before beta launch. AI-generated template errors are not acceptable.
- Template staleness: as athletes’ fitness evolves, the template intensity may need recalibration. An FTP re-test prompt at weeks 4 and 8 is the initial mitigation.
- Nutrition advice liability: coaching notes framed as suggestions, not medical advice. Disclaimer in onboarding.
- Cold start confidence: in the first 1–2 weeks, the memory_digest is sparse. Max must communicate appropriate confidence levels. “I’m still learning your patterns — check back in after a few more rides.”
- Token cost at scale: the 580-token prompt target must be validated against actual Claude API costs at projected user volumes. Background summary jobs may need optimisation if compute costs are prohibitive.

### 12.3 Key Decisions Still Open

- Pricing: free personal tool vs. freemium vs. $12–15/month subscription?
- Mobile-first vs. web-first: dashboard suits desktop; daily coaching note and race day reference suits mobile.
- Template curation process: who builds and validates the templates? Founder only for Phase 1, or bring in an experienced coach as an advisor?
- FTP estimation: for users who don’t know their FTP, what is the onboarding protocol? RPE-based estimation ride, or a deferred first-week test?
- Whoop-less fallback quality: how confident is the coaching note when recovery signal is manual only? Should Max explicitly flag lower confidence in the coaching note text?

## Appendix A: Glossary

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| TSS | Training Stress Score — a measure of workout load combining intensity and duration |
| CTL | Chronic Training Load — rolling 42-day average TSS; represents fitness |
| ATL | Acute Training Load — rolling 7-day average TSS; represents fatigue |
| TSB | Training Stress Balance (Form) — CTL minus ATL; positive = fresh, negative = fatigued |
| FTP | Functional Threshold Power — max power a cyclist can sustain for approximately 60 minutes |
| HRV | Heart Rate Variability — key biomarker of recovery and readiness |
| VO₂ Max | Maximal oxygen uptake — the gold standard metric for aerobic fitness. Also Max’s namesake. |
| Whoop Recovery | A 0–100% score reflecting readiness to train based on HRV, sleep, and resting HR |
| Zone 2 | Aerobic endurance zone — roughly 56–75% of FTP; builds mitochondrial density |
| Athlete Data Layer | Breakaway’s structured database of all wearable inputs, training rides, and pre-computed context summaries |
| Context Summary | A pre-computed, token-efficient data object passed to Claude instead of raw table data |
| Memory Digest | A compressed summary of longitudinal athlete patterns — the core of the compounding moat |
| Template Library | The curated library of race-specific coaching plan templates. Phase 1 contains Berkeley Hills Road Race. |
| Graceful Degradation | Breakaway’s design principle: every user gets a meaningful product regardless of which devices they own |
| Option A | The confirmed Phase 1 plan approach: pre-built templates adjusted by AI, not AI-generated from scratch |
